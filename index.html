About Me

Currently: LSU Senior · Cyber Risk focus

Why a rocket?

I'm Colin Lee, a senior at LSU studying Information Systems and Analytics with a concentration in Cyber Risk and a minor in Computer Science. I've been into tech for as long as I can remember. As a kid I took things apart to see how they worked, set up the sprinkler systems around our house, and ran all of our home networking. As I grew up I kept learning, and when SpaceX took off it pulled me into aviation too. SpaceX is a technology company at heart, so it all tied back to what I already loved: computers, space, and IT. Starlink brings those together, with satellites, networking, and security keeping people connected from a backyard in New Orleans to an airliner at 35,000 feet. That's why my site is a rocket.

Driven by a passion for technology, cybersecurity, and problem-solving, I aim to contribute to secure and innovative digital solutions. Geaux Tigers!

Mission notes

Motion is reduced, so the flight is described instead of played.

  1. The ship quick-disconnect swings clear. Raptors light with the deluge, and the stack lifts off the mount.
  2. Through max-Q to MECO. Hot staging: the ship lights while it is still attached, then pulls away through the vented ring.
  3. The booster flips, boosts back, and is caught by the chopsticks.
  4. On orbit the payload door opens. Satellites slide out one at a time and unfold into the notes below, taken from this site.
  5. Deorbit burn, plasma on the heat shield, belly-flop, flip, landing burn, and a catch on a second tower. Then the view returns to the pad.
